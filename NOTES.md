# NOTES.md — Variant A (kitchen/food dad pun)

## Concept
A food/kitchen item with a pun on its name or job. Must be a FRESH pun — no
famous lines ("Hi hungry, I'm dad", "I'm egg-cited", "nacho cheese", "lettuce
in", "olive you", "pasta la vista", "you butter believe it").

## Version log (v1–v5, all one line, 100–140 bytes)

### v1 — toaster (PICKED)
> My toaster filed for divorce this morning — it said I'd let things go stale and never buttered it up anymore.
- 109 chars / 111 bytes.
- Two linked puns (stale / buttered it up), punchline is the last phrase.

### v2 — spaghetti
> The spaghetti got a job at the bank — it heard the pay was great, but it just couldn't handle the penne pressure.
- 113 chars / 115 bytes.
- "Penne pressure" is cute, but the bank setup is longer and the pun is a
  single word swap — less of a scene than v1.

### v3 — blender
> My blender got fired for stirring up drama — HR said it just couldn't stop blending in with the wrong crowd.
- 108 chars / 110 bytes.
- "Blending in" works, but "stirring up drama" is a stock phrase and the
  turn is weaker than v1's divorce framing.

### v4 — cheddar
> The cheddar went to therapy last week — it kept feeling grated, so the counselor said, 'Stop shredding yourself apart.'
- 119 chars / 121 bytes.
- "Feeling grated" is a decent fresh pun, but the quoted punchline splits
  the line into two voices and slows the landing.

### v5 — salt & pepper
> The salt and pepper had a fight last night — pepper said salt was too salty, so salt told it to stop being so extra.
- 116 chars / 118 bytes.
- "Being so extra" is fun, but "too salty" leans on a common slang pun and
  the joke has no single sharp turn.

## Why v1 won
- One concrete scene (a toaster filing for divorce) + one surprising turn,
  both inside the single line — matches the winning skeleton from the
  refpack: named specific thing + literal wordplay → punchline that inverts
  the expectation in the same line.
- Punchline lands at the very end ("buttered it up"), no wasted words, and
  it rolls off the tongue when said out loud — a dad would actually say it.
- Fresh: the divorce framing is not a famous dad joke; "stale" + "buttered
  it up" are two linked puns rather than one recycled line.
- 109 chars / 111 bytes — uses most of the byte budget without padding.

## Revision (after first successful run)
- Self-critique vs the judge brief: v1's ending "never buttered it up
  anymore" trails off — "anymore" adds a syllable after the punchline and
  softens the landing. Tightened to "never once buttered it up": the pun
  ("buttered it up") is now the final phrase, and "never once" adds a beat
  of rhythm before it. Final: 107 chars / 109 bytes, still in the 100–140
  window.
